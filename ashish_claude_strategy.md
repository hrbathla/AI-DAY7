## 🚫 Biggest Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Using Opus for every task** — It's slower and wastes message quota on simple work like captions or quick Q&A.

2. **Writing vague prompts** — As an AI educator, you know this: "write a LinkedIn post" gets generic output. Add context, audience, tone, and goal every time.

3. **Using Max effort for standard learning tasks** — It adds latency with minimal benefit for everyday HR analytics questions.

4. **Starting a new chat for every task** — Carry context forward within a session for related work (e.g. drafting a post + its visual brief in one conversation).

5. **Not using role prompting for yourself** — You teach it to others; apply it to your own prompts for HR, strategy, and content work.

## 💡 Key Learnings from This Strategy Session

### Learning 1: The Model Matters Less Than the Prompt
The single biggest lever is prompt quality — not model choice. Sonnet 4.6 with a well-structured role prompt consistently outperforms Opus 4.6 with a vague one. Apply your own curriculum to your own workflow.

### Learning 2: Build a 3-Tier Model Habit
Think of your models like tools in a toolkit:
- **Haiku** = a pen (fast, light, good for quick marks)
- **Sonnet** = a laptop (your daily driver, handles most professional work)
- **Opus** = a specialist consultant (bring in only when the problem demands it)

### Learning 3: Effort ≠ Time Spent
Choosing "Max effort" doesn't mean spending more time — it means Claude will reason more carefully before responding. Use it selectively on outputs that go to leadership or public audiences.

### Learning 4: Context Continuity is Underrated
Keeping a conversation open for a full work session (e.g. planning a post, drafting it, then creating a visual brief) gives Claude running context — which produces noticeably more coherent output than restarting fresh each time.

### Learning 5: Your Dual Role is Your Advantage
Being both a learner AND a teacher of AI tools gives you a unique feedback loop. When you apply a prompting technique to your own work and it succeeds, that becomes authentic content for your LinkedIn challenge — you're learning by doing, and doing by teaching.
